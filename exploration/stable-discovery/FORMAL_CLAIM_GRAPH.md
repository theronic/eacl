# Formal claim graph and remaining refinement seams

Status: exploration-only assurance map. This file is ignored by Git and makes
no release claim.

## Interpretation

`proved-model` means the stated relation is discharged for the abstract types
and assumptions named in the row. It does not transfer automatically to
Clojure, ClojureScript, an adapter, a cursor codec, a JVM heap, or a storage
service. `checked-executable` means an independent project-local executable
model or mutation control passed. `measured` is workload evidence, not a
theorem. `unknown` is a required gate, not a euphemism for confidence.

The proof architecture deliberately follows runtime boundaries. A semantic
change should normally touch one leaf and verify in under two seconds. If a
claim needs whole-history reconstruction or unrelated host state, the default
response is to repair the algorithm/state boundary rather than weaken the
claim or tune the solver.

## Current verification topology

- Every Dafny and TLC file is discovered from the project-local exploration
  directory and must be declared exactly once in `verify-fast.sh`.
- Aggregate Dafny obligation count is independently guarded; deleting a leaf
  cannot manufacture a faster pass.
- Dafny proof escape hatches are rejected, and the exact TLA+ constant/domain
  assumption boundary is fingerprinted; adding a premise cannot silently
  manufacture a faster pass.
- Affected leaves have a five-second hard wall ceiling and target under two
  seconds; the complete gate has a ten-second hard wall ceiling and target
  under seven seconds.
- The new count leaf verifies in about one second. Three consecutive complete
  536-obligation gates took about 7.8 seconds: the hard ceiling passes, while
  the soft seven-second target is presently missed and must not be relabeled.
- Four independent Dafny batches, the fixed-seed executable campaign, and five
  isolated TLC worker batches overlap.
- Formal, executable, source-refinement, cryptographic, backend, and benchmark
  evidence remain separate tiers.

## Confidence conclusion

The abstract semantic architecture has no currently known unresolved choice,
but end-to-end release confidence is not 100 percent. The largest remaining
risks are not hard mathematics: they are compiler/source refinement, adapter
scan qualification, cursor codec/authentication, concrete scheduler/cache
ownership, and frozen replacement-engine performance. Those are exactly the
next gates. A fast proof is being used to expose and simplify runtime state;
it is not being used to relabel untested source as correct or fast.
